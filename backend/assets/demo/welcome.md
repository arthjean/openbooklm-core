# Welcome to OpenbookLM

## Your documents, ready to answer

OpenbookLM is a tool that lets you have a conversation with your own documents. Upload a PDF, paste a web article, or type some notes, then ask questions in plain language. Every answer comes with citations that link back to the exact passage in your source material, so you can always verify what the AI tells you.

Unlike a general-purpose chatbot, OpenbookLM never makes things up from its general training data. It only answers based on the documents you provide. If the answer isn't in your sources, it will tell you rather than guessing.

## How is it different from ChatGPT?

When you ask ChatGPT a question, it draws on everything it learned during training: billions of web pages, books, and articles. That's powerful, but it means you can never be sure where an answer came from. ChatGPT can also "hallucinate," confidently stating things that sound right but aren't.

OpenbookLM works differently. It reads only the documents you upload to a notebook. When it answers your question, it points you to the specific paragraph or sentence it used. You see a numbered citation next to each claim, and you can click it to jump straight to the original text. This makes OpenbookLM ideal for work where accuracy matters: research, legal review, studying, or any situation where you need to trust and verify the source of information.

Think of it this way: ChatGPT is like asking a knowledgeable friend who read a lot but might misremember details. OpenbookLM is like having a research assistant who has your specific documents open and shows you exactly where they found each answer.

## How it works

Using OpenbookLM follows a simple three-step pattern:

**Step 1: Add your sources.** Upload PDFs, paste web page URLs, type or paste text, or import Markdown files. You can add multiple sources to a single notebook to build a rich knowledge base. OpenbookLM supports documents in any language.

**Step 2: Ask questions.** Type your question in the chat, just like you would ask a colleague. You can ask factual questions ("What does section 3.2 say about delivery timelines?"), request summaries ("Summarize the key findings"), make comparisons ("How do the two proposals differ on pricing?"), or explore ideas ("What are the main arguments for and against this approach?").

**Step 3: Get cited answers.** The AI reads through your sources, finds the most relevant passages, and writes a clear answer. Each claim is backed by a numbered citation. Click any citation to see the exact source text it came from. No guessing, no black box, just transparent, verifiable answers.

## What you can do with OpenbookLM

OpenbookLM is designed for anyone who works with documents and needs reliable, sourced answers.

**Research and study.** Upload lecture notes, textbook chapters, or research papers. Ask questions to test your understanding, generate summaries, or find connections across multiple sources. Students use it to prepare for exams; researchers use it to quickly navigate dense literature.

**Professional document review.** Drop in contracts, reports, policy documents, or technical specifications. Ask specific questions about clauses, requirements, or data points instead of manually scanning hundreds of pages. Lawyers, consultants, and analysts use OpenbookLM to cut review time dramatically.

**Content analysis.** Paste articles, blog posts, or transcripts. Ask the AI to compare viewpoints, extract key arguments, or identify themes across multiple pieces. Journalists and content strategists use it to synthesize large volumes of material quickly.

**Personal knowledge management.** Import your notes, saved articles, or reading highlights. Writers, academics, and lifelong learners use OpenbookLM to build a searchable, conversational archive of everything they've read and written. Ask questions months later and get answers with references back to your original notes.

## Choose your AI model

One feature that makes OpenbookLM unique is the ability to choose which AI model answers your questions. Different models have different strengths: some are faster, some are more thorough, and some are better at nuanced reasoning. OpenbookLM supports models from several providers, including Anthropic Claude, OpenAI GPT, and Mistral.

You don't need to understand the technical differences to get started. Just pick one and ask your question. You can always switch models later to compare how they handle the same question. The citations always come from your documents regardless of which model you choose, so accuracy stays consistent.

If you have your own API keys from these providers, you can even bring them to OpenbookLM and use your own accounts directly. This feature is called "Bring Your Own Key" (BYOK). It means no vendor lock-in and full control over your AI usage.

## Supported document types

OpenbookLM accepts the following source types:

- **PDF files**: research papers, reports, contracts, ebooks, scanned documents
- **Web pages**: paste any URL and OpenbookLM will extract and index the content
- **Plain text**: copy and paste text directly, or type your own notes
- **Markdown files**: import structured documents with formatting preserved
- **Word documents**: upload .docx files from Microsoft Word or Google Docs exports
- **EPUB files**: import ebooks and long-form publications

You can mix and match source types within a single notebook. For example, you might combine a PDF research paper, a related web article, and your own notes, then ask questions that draw from all three.

## Get started

You're reading this document inside a demo notebook. This is exactly how OpenbookLM works: the AI has read this page and can answer questions about it.

Try clicking one of the suggested questions below the chat input, or type your own question about anything you just read. Watch how the answer includes numbered citations that link back to specific sections of this document.

When you're ready, create a new notebook from the sidebar and add your own documents. Each notebook is a separate workspace: sources in one notebook do not affect answers in another, so you can organize your work by project, topic, or however you like. Your first cited answer is moments away.
